Heir Locator & Probate Genealogy Research

Documented heirs, ready for the people who decide the claim.

Unclaimed estates, escheated property, and heir-locator cases all turn on one question: who, documented by record, is actually entitled to inherit. KVSV researches and organizes heir lines — building the sourced family tree an estate, attorney, or administrator needs — without ever making the legal determination of who inherits, which stays with the court or qualified counsel.

Illustrative Example: From Question to Next Step

A customer receives a letter about a possible unclaimed inheritance and needs to prove the family connection. The next step is building the documented chain, generation by generation, from the customer back to the named decedent — each link resting on a specific vital or probate record, not on family memory alone. The finished packet either supports the claim with citations or shows exactly where the chain currently breaks, which is what the administering attorney will need to know either way.

Illustrative example only — generic and fictional, not a real case, client, or testimonial.

Who This Service Is For

Individuals notified of a possible unclaimed inheritance who need their heir line documented; attorneys, administrators, or title companies needing a sourced heir genealogy; families researching whether they may be entitled heirs to an estate.

What KVSV Helps You Do

  • Build a sourced heir line connecting a claimant to a decedent through documented relationships.
  • Identify all documented potential heirs in a given generation, not just the one researching.
  • Organize supporting records — vital records, probate files, deeds — into a citation-ready packet.
  • Flag any gap in the documented chain that would need further evidence.
  • Prepare the heir research file for attorney, court, or administrator submission.

What This Service Helps You Do and The Value You Receive

This service helps you arrive at a legal or administrative heirship process with your documented family connection already organized and sourced, instead of starting from scratch. The value you receive is a citation-ready heir-line file — every claimed relationship backed by a record — prepared for exactly the process (court, attorney, administrator) that will actually decide the claim.

What You Receive

  • A sourced heir-line chart connecting the claimant to the decedent.
  • Identification of other documented potential heirs in the relevant generations.
  • A citation-ready packet of supporting vital, probate, and land records.
  • A clear statement of any gap in the documented chain and what would close it.

Process

  1. Establish the target relationship — the decedent and the claimed connection are identified and scoped.
  2. Build and source the chain — each generational link is documented with vital, probate, or other records.
  3. Package for submission — the sourced heir line and supporting records are organized for the relevant legal or administrative process.

Human Review, Privacy, and Limitations

This is research support only. KVSV documents and sources family relationships for heirship purposes; it does not determine legal heirship, does not represent claimants in any legal or administrative proceeding, and refers all such determinations to the appropriate court, administrator, or qualified licensed attorney.

Proprietary boundary: Modeled after the research discipline of the KVSV platform and research portal, without disclosing or reproducing proprietary KVSV systems, internal workbooks, databases, backend systems, communications, or daily operating methods.

FAQ

Can you tell me if I'll receive the inheritance?

No — that determination belongs to the court, estate administrator, or attorney handling the matter; we document and source the family connection, not the legal outcome.

What if I'm not actually an heir?

That's reported honestly — a documented negative finding protects you from pursuing a claim the records don't support.

Do you handle the legal claim process?

No — we prepare the sourced genealogical research; filing and pursuing the actual claim is handled by you or your attorney.
