Custom Research Consultation & Project Scoping

Not sure where to start? That's a fair place to start.

Some research questions don't fit neatly into one service — a tangled multi-generational puzzle, a family with several open threads at once, or simply not knowing which of KVSV's services actually fits. This is a scoping consultation: a conversation to understand your family history goals and lay out a realistic, honest research plan before any billable work begins.

Illustrative Example: From Question to Next Step

A customer has a shoebox of documents, a DNA test, three family stories, and no idea where to start. The next step isn't picking a service at random — it's a scoping conversation that sorts the shoebox into what's actually documentary evidence, identifies that the DNA question and the documentary question are separate threads, and produces a written plan: start with the documentary side, revisit the DNA side once a working tree exists to test it against.

Illustrative example only — generic and fictional, not a real case, client, or testimonial.

Who This Service Is For

Anyone unsure which KVSV service fits their situation; people with a complex, multi-threaded research question; new customers wanting a plan and a realistic expectation before committing to a specific research track.

What KVSV Helps You Do

  • Talk through your family history goals and what you already know.
  • Identify which KVSV service, or combination of services, actually fits your question.
  • Set a realistic expectation for what's likely findable and what may not be.
  • Sequence a multi-part research question into a sensible order of operations.
  • Provide a written project scope before any specific research track begins.

What This Service Helps You Do and The Value You Receive

This service helps you avoid guessing which service to book, or starting research in the wrong order for a complex question. The value you receive is a clear, honest project scope — what's realistic, what sequence makes sense, and which specific KVSV services actually apply — before you commit to a specific research track.

What You Receive

  • A scoping conversation covering your goals and existing research.
  • A written project scope naming the recommended service or sequence of services.
  • An honest assessment of what's realistically findable for your specific question.
  • A sequencing plan for multi-part or multi-generational research questions.

Process

  1. Talk it through — your goals, known information, and open questions are discussed.
  2. Assess and sequence — a realistic plan is built, naming which services apply and in what order.
  3. Deliver the scope — a written project scope is provided before any specific research track begins.

Work Examples

Illustrative and generic — not testimonials or real client cases.

  • A customer with five separate open family questions receives a written plan sequencing them by which is most likely to unlock the others first.
  • A customer unsure whether their question is a legal, DNA, or documentary-research matter receives a scope that correctly routes it to Land, Probate & Heirs Property Research with a note on which parts need an attorney instead.

Human Review, Privacy, and Limitations

This is a scoping conversation and planning service; it does not itself perform research, and any research plan produced is a recommendation, not a guarantee of what will ultimately be found.

Proprietary boundary: Modeled after the research discipline of the KVSV platform and research portal, without disclosing or reproducing proprietary KVSV systems, internal workbooks, databases, backend systems, communications, or daily operating methods.

FAQ

Does this consultation cost the same as a research service?

This is a scoping conversation and written plan, distinct from a specific research engagement; ask directly for current details on how it's structured.

What if my question doesn't fit any of KVSV's listed services?

That's exactly what this consultation is for — naming the closest fit, flagging what's outside KVSV's scope, and pointing you toward the right kind of help either way.

Can this replace Service 19's expert review?

No — this is for scoping new or multi-part research; Service 19 is specifically for reviewing research that already exists. Often the two work well back to back.
