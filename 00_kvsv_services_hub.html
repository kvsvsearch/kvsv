KVSV Services & Research Hub

Ten research services. One evidence discipline. Every record traced to its source.

KVSV is a family-records research practice built around Coosa County, Alabama and the Coosa Empire, Creek and Cherokee ancestry, Freedmen's Bureau documentation, mound-builder and contact-era history, and the land, probate, and heirs-property records tied to all of it. This page is the index for the ten research services described in full on their own pages below, plus direct links to the live KVSV platform — its public archive and search tool, its narrative articles, its oral-history and private-portal features, and how to contact or contribute to the project. Every service on this hub applies the same evidence-first discipline: define the question, search the record systems that can actually answer it, weigh what each source really supports, document conflicts and gaps honestly, and label documented fact, family tradition, and open questions as what they are — never blended together.

How KVSV's Research Discipline Works

Every entry in the KVSV archive is organized against a Collect / Analyze / Package workflow and rated on a transparent, five-tier evidentiary scale — from certified or official primary sources at the top, down through cross-confirmed secondary sources, family- or researcher-stated information, and unverified leads or working theories at the bottom. The reasoning behind each tier is shown, not buried inside an opaque score. This is the same discipline applied across all thirty services on this hub: a finding is only as strong as the record that supports it, and the archive says so plainly for every single entry.

That discipline runs on a base of courthouse deeds, federal census and land-patent records, tribal enrollment rolls, Freedmen's Bureau documentation, and logged oral testimony — cross-referenced by family line rather than siloed by record type, because most real research questions cut across all of them at once.

Family Lines & Historical Focus

KVSV's research concentrates on Coosa County, Alabama and the wider footprint of the historical Coosa Empire — including Mound Builder history, the era of early Spanish, French, and English contact and exploration, and the treaties and demographic shifts that followed. Within that footprint, the practice traces Creek and Cherokee ancestry, African American lines that cross enslavement-era and Freedmen's Bureau record systems, and the land patents, probate files, and heirs-property questions that connect all of these family lines to specific parcels of land over time. Services 06, 07, and 08 are built specifically for the harder record systems this focus runs through.

Several of the newer, more specialized services extend this same regional focus in specific directions: Service 27 researches the Coosa Empire and Mound Builder era directly as historical context; Service 24 applies Alabama and Coosa County-specific record knowledge to any search; Service 25 searches the Freedmen's Bureau records that bridge enslavement-era and post-emancipation documentation; Service 26 searches historical tribal rolls as documentary history, never as a citizenship or enrollment determination; and Service 28 searches the slave schedules and estate records that are often the only pre-1870 record of an enslaved ancestor's existence. Every other service on this hub applies the same evidentiary discipline to any family history question, in or out of this specific regional footprint.

Legal & Title Research Context (Not Legal Advice)

Land, probate, and heirs-property questions — covered on Service 06, and extended by Service 15 (Court Records & Litigation History) and Service 23 (Heir Locator & Probate Genealogy) — sit next to real legal questions: heirship, title, and partition. Heirs-property law varies by state and by the specific facts of an estate, and KVSV does not track, cite, or apply case law or statute on this hub or on any service page. What KVSV does is organize the documentary trail — deed chains, probate files, timelines — into a referral-ready file so that when a legal question does need answering, it goes to a qualified licensed attorney or title professional with the research already done, not before. Nothing on this hub or in any linked service page is legal advice, a title opinion, or a legal determination of any kind.

FAQ

Which service do I actually need?

Most people start with Family History Research (01) or Genealogy Research Services (02) if they're early in the process, or jump straight to a specialized track — Land, Probate & Heirs Property (06), Military, Census & Immigration (07), or African American, Indigenous & Mixed-Heritage (08) — if they already know which record systems their question runs through.

Can I combine more than one service?

Yes. Most real research questions touch more than one track — a land question often needs Historical Document Research (05) to read the deed itself, and a difficult family line often pairs Oral History (09) with African American, Indigenous & Mixed-Heritage Research (08). Each service page names related services for exactly this reason.

Does KVSV provide legal advice or make legal determinations?

No. Research organizes documentary evidence and prepares files for review; it never provides legal advice, title opinions, heirship determinations, or legal representation. Legal and title questions are always referred to a qualified licensed attorney or title professional — see Service 06 for the full boundary language.

Does KVSV determine tribal citizenship, enrollment, or identity?

No. Citizenship and enrollment are determined solely by each nation or tribe through its own process. KVSV organizes documentation and testimony and clearly labels what is documented fact, what is tradition, and what remains an open question — see Service 08 for the full boundary language.

Is anything on this hub connected to a live account, form, or payment system?

There's no separate form, payment processor, or account system built into these pages. The "Request Information" and "Schedule a Consultation" buttons on every page open KVSV's live Contact page in a new tab, and that's where an actual inquiry happens. No analytics or tracking scripts run on any of these thirty-one pages themselves.

Do you offer DNA analysis?

Yes — Service 11, DNA Match Analysis & Genetic Genealogy, organizes match lists into clusters and pairs them with documentary research. DNA results are always treated as a lead to test against records, not a stand-alone proof.

Can you help with an adoption search?

Yes — Service 16, Adoption & Biological Family Search, explains your state's specific records-access rules and organizes available and public information, at whatever pace feels right to you.

Do you handle court records, heir searches, or unclaimed-property cases?

Yes — Service 15 (Court Records & Litigation History), Service 06 (Land, Probate & Heirs Property), and Service 23 (Heir Locator & Probate Genealogy) all build sourced, citation-ready documentary files for exactly this kind of question. None of them provide legal advice or make the legal determination themselves — that's always referred to a qualified licensed attorney.
