Historical Maps & Land Survey (GIS) Research

A plat map draws the line words alone can't.

A deed can describe a parcel in metes and bounds that mean little until you see it drawn. KVSV works with historical plat maps, land surveys, and GIS tools to place your family's land in real geography — showing boundaries, neighbors, and changes over time that a written description alone leaves hard to picture.

Illustrative Example: From Question to Next Step

A family has a deed describing land by metes and bounds that mean nothing to a modern reader. The next step is translation, not more reading: the description is plotted using the original survey's bearings and distances, then compared against a modern county parcel map. What emerges is a real, visible boundary — and often a name for the family living on the neighboring plat the deed only referenced as 'adjoining lands of.'

Illustrative example only — generic and fictional, not a real case, client, or testimonial.

Who This Service Is For

Researchers trying to visualize where an ancestor's land actually sat; families comparing old plat descriptions to modern parcels; anyone whose land or heirs-property research needs a map, not just a deed.

What KVSV Helps You Do

  • Locate historical plat maps, survey plats, and GLO (General Land Office) records for a parcel.
  • Translate metes-and-bounds deed language into a plotted boundary.
  • Compare historical parcel maps to modern county GIS parcel data.
  • Identify neighboring landowners from adjoining plat records.
  • Track how a parcel's boundaries or ownership divisions changed across multiple surveys.

What This Service Helps You Do and The Value You Receive

This service helps you see your family's land, not just read about it. The value you receive is a plotted map — historical and, where possible, overlaid on modern GIS parcel data — that turns a deed's written description into a boundary you can actually look at, along with the neighboring landowners the same plat records reveal.

What You Receive

  • A plotted map of the parcel from its deed or survey description.
  • Located historical plat maps or GLO land patent records.
  • A modern GIS overlay comparison where county data allows it.
  • A list of neighboring landowners identified from adjoining records.

Process

  1. Gather the descriptions — deed language, survey references, and any known plat numbers are collected.
  2. Plot and locate — boundaries are translated onto a map using historical and, where available, modern GIS data.
  3. Compare and document — the plotted parcel is compared across available surveys, with neighbors and changes noted.

Work Examples

Illustrative and generic — not testimonials or real client cases.

  • Comparing two GLO patent surveys twenty years apart reveals the original parcel was later split between two sons — a division no deed index alone made clear.

Human Review, Privacy, and Limitations

This service plots historical and documentary boundary information for research purposes; it does not constitute a legal survey, does not establish current property lines, and does not replace a licensed surveyor or title professional for any matter with legal or financial consequences.

FAQ

Can you tell me if my family still owns this land?

Current ownership is a matter for the county assessor or recorder's current records; we plot historical boundaries and can point you to where to check present-day ownership.

What if the original survey markers no longer exist?

Historical plats and GLO records often let us plot a reasonably accurate boundary even without surviving physical markers; where precision matters legally, a licensed surveyor is the next step.

Do you work with GLO land patents?

Yes — General Land Office patent records and their accompanying survey plats are a core source for this service.
