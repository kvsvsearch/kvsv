Genealogy Research Services

Research built on evidence, not guesswork.

Genealogy succeeds when every conclusion rests on a cited source. KVSV’s research services apply a disciplined, evidence-first method: define the question, search the right records, weigh what they actually say, and document everything. You get organized research you can verify, extend, or hand to a professional — never a tree built on unexamined hints.

Illustrative Example: From Question to Next Step

A customer asks, “Who was my great-grandmother’s father?” The next step is narrowing: establish her maiden name and residence at marriage from the marriage record, then test candidate fathers in that county’s household structures. The deliverable is not a triumphant name — it’s a cited shortlist with the strongest candidate marked probable, not proven, and the record that could settle it named.

Illustrative example only — generic and fictional, not a real case, client, or testimonial.

Who This Service Is For

Customers who want methodical, documented research on a specific question — a parentage puzzle, an immigrant origin, a missing generation — and who value accuracy over speed or volume.

What KVSV Helps You Do

  • Define a focused, answerable research question.
  • Build a source-based research plan before searching begins.
  • Weigh direct and indirect evidence and record conflicting information.
  • Maintain a research log so every step is reproducible.
  • Package findings for your own use or for a professional’s review.

What This Service Helps You Do and The Value You Receive

This service helps you put one hard genealogy question through a disciplined evidence process instead of trial-and-error searching. The value you receive is a defensible written analysis: what the records show, how strong the evidence is, what conflicts remain, and exactly which next searches could resolve them.

What You Receive

  • A written research question and scope statement.
  • A cited evidence analysis with conflicts clearly presented.
  • A complete research log of sources searched.
  • Prioritized recommendations for further work.

Process

  1. Define the question — we narrow a broad goal into a testable research question.
  2. Search and analyze — records are gathered, transcribed, and weighed against each other.
  3. Report and recommend — you receive an evidence analysis, the research log, and next steps.

Work Examples

Illustrative and generic — not testimonials or real client cases.

  • An evidence analysis weighs two census entries against a death certificate to assess which supports a stated parentage; both positions and their weaknesses are documented.
  • A research log for one immigrant ancestor lists eleven sources across three repositories, with transcriptions and relevance notes for each.

Human Review, Privacy, and Limitations

Genealogy research produces cited findings, leads, and honest uncertainty — not guaranteed answers. It does not establish legal parentage, authenticate documents, or replace DNA analysis or professional legal review where those are required.

Proprietary boundary: Modeled after the research discipline of the KVSV platform and research portal, without disclosing or reproducing proprietary KVSV systems, internal workbooks, databases, backend systems, communications, or daily operating methods.

FAQ

What’s the difference between a lead and a finding?

A lead is an unverified pointer worth checking; a finding is a cited statement tied to a specific source. We label each one.

Can you take over my existing tree?

Yes — existing material is reviewed, sourced claims are kept, and unsourced claims are flagged for verification.

How do you handle brick walls?

By changing the question’s angle — neighbors, associates, records of indirect kin — and documenting each attempt, including failures.
