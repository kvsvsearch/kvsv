DNA Match Analysis & Genetic Genealogy

A DNA match is a lead, not a conclusion.

DNA testing can point toward relatives paper records never named — and it can also mislead when read without documentary context. KVSV helps you organize match lists, shared-DNA amounts, and triangulated segments, then pairs that genetic evidence with records research so a match becomes a documented connection, not a guess.

Illustrative Example: From Question to Next Step

A customer has 60 DNA matches and no idea what most of them mean. The next step is sorting, not searching: cM ranges get organized into likely relationship tiers, shared matches get clustered, and each cluster is paired with a documentary hypothesis. One cluster points toward a specific county in the 1900 census — that's where the record search starts, not from all 60 matches at once.

Illustrative example only — generic and fictional, not a real case, client, or testimonial.

Who This Service Is For

People with AncestryDNA, 23andMe, MyHeritage, or FamilyTreeDNA results who have matches they can't place; researchers using DNA to test a specific paper-trail hypothesis; anyone building a genetic network alongside a documentary tree.

What KVSV Helps You Do

  • Organize match lists by shared cM and probable relationship range.
  • Group matches into clusters that point to a shared ancestral couple.
  • Pair a genetic cluster with the documentary research needed to name the connection.
  • Flag matches that need a specialist — complex endogamy, adoption cases, or disputed parentage.
  • Keep genetic findings clearly labeled as genetic, separate from documentary proof.

What This Service Helps You Do and The Value You Receive

This service helps you turn a match list into an organized research asset instead of a confusing spreadsheet. The value you receive is a cluster analysis that tells you which matches point to which branch of your tree — and exactly which record search could confirm the connection the DNA is pointing toward.

What You Receive

  • A shared-cM and relationship-range organized match list.
  • Cluster groupings with a working hypothesis for each cluster's shared ancestor.
  • A prioritized list of record searches to confirm each hypothesis.
  • Referral flags for matches needing a certified genetic genealogist.

Process

  1. Organize the match list — shared cM, trees, and notes are compiled into one working file.
  2. Cluster and hypothesize — matches are grouped, and a documentary hypothesis is proposed for each cluster.
  3. Test against records — the hypothesis is checked against census, vital, and land records, with results reported plainly.

Work Examples

Illustrative and generic — not testimonials or real client cases.

  • Twelve unplaced matches cluster into two groups; census research identifies the shared great-great-grandparents behind the larger cluster, cited and confirmed.
  • A 40-cM match resists explanation until a marriage record reveals a previously unknown second marriage, which resolves three other unplaced matches at once.

Human Review, Privacy, and Limitations

DNA analysis here organizes match data and proposes documentary hypotheses; it does not itself prove a specific relationship, determine legal parentage, or replace interpretation by a certified genetic genealogist for complex, disputed, or emotionally sensitive cases. Genetic findings are always labeled as genetic, separate from documentary proof.

Proprietary boundary: Modeled after the research discipline of the KVSV platform and research portal, without disclosing or reproducing proprietary KVSV systems, internal workbooks, databases, backend systems, communications, or daily operating methods.

FAQ

Can you tell me exactly how I'm related to a match?

We can build a strong documentary hypothesis from clustering and records, but confirming an exact relationship sometimes needs additional testing or a specialist's review.

Do you interpret ethnicity estimates?

Ethnicity estimates are population-level statistics, not proof of specific ancestry; we treat them as context, not evidence for a specific ancestor.

What about close, unexpected matches (NPE situations)?

Those are handled with extra care and referred to a specialist experienced in unexpected-parentage cases; we won't rush a sensitive finding.
