Adoption & Biological Family Search

Every family search starts with respect for everyone in it.

Adoption research means working with records that exist specifically to protect privacy — sealed files, non-identifying information, and state-specific access rules. KVSV helps you understand what's realistically searchable, organize what you already have, and identify the right process and records for your specific state and situation, at whatever pace feels right to you.

Illustrative Example: From Question to Next Step

A customer has only a birth date and a state of birth from an adoption decree. The next step is understanding that state's specific access rules — some states open original birth certificates to adult adoptees, most don't automatically — and identifying the correct registry to petition. Historical record research runs alongside that process, not ahead of it.

Who This Service Is For

Adoptees searching for birth family information; birth parents or relatives searching for a person who was adopted; family members trying to document an adoption that happened generations ago.

What KVSV Helps You Do

  • Explain what adoption records are typically open, restricted, or sealed in a given state and era.
  • Organize non-identifying information already available to you.
  • Identify the correct court, agency, or registry for a records request.
  • Search public and historical records — census, city directories, court dockets — for corroborating detail.
  • Pair documentary research with DNA leads where you've chosen to test.

What This Service Helps You Do and The Value You Receive

This service helps you understand the realistic path forward for your specific state and situation, and organizes whatever documentary trail is available to you. The value you receive is clarity on the process and records that actually apply to your case, plus whatever historical corroboration public records can add — at a pace you control.

What You Receive

  • A plain-language explanation of your state's adoption-records access rules.
  • An organized file of the non-identifying or available information you already have.
  • Identification of the correct agency, court, or state registry for further requests.
  • Any corroborating detail found in public historical records.

Process

  1. Understand your situation — your state, the adoption era, and what information you already have are reviewed together.
  2. Organize and identify next steps — available information is organized and the correct agency or registry contact is identified.
  3. Research and corroborate — public records are searched for anything that corroborates or adds context, always at your pace.

Human Review, Privacy, and Limitations

This service explains records-access rules and organizes available and public information; it does not unseal legal records, perform DNA testing, make contact on your behalf, or provide legal or counseling services. Adoption search can be emotionally significant, and pacing is always led by you.

Proprietary boundary: Modeled after the research discipline of the KVSV platform and research portal, without disclosing or reproducing proprietary KVSV systems, internal workbooks, databases, backend systems, communications, or daily operating methods.

FAQ

Can you unseal my adoption records for me?

No — unsealing sealed records is a legal process specific to your state and requires the appropriate court or registry, not a research service. We help you identify exactly which one applies to you.

Will you contact a birth family member for me?

We don't make that contact; when you're ready, that's a step you take yourself, and we can help you prepare what you know beforehand.

How fast can this move?

As fast or as slow as you want. There's no requirement to move faster than feels right, and the research pauses whenever you need it to.
